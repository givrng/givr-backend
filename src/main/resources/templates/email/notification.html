The volunteer who never showed up

Another story from behind the scenes at Givr 💙

Hi Name,

Every volunteer opportunity starts with hope.

An organisation creates a project.

Applications start coming in.

Someone reviews every application, reaches out to volunteers, creates a WhatsApp group, assigns roles, plans logistics, and begins to picture what the day will look like.

Then the day comes.

And someone doesn't show up.

Sometimes it's one person.

Sometimes it's half the team—or even more than that.

The interesting part is that most people don't do it because they don't care.

Life happens.

Plans change.

Emergencies come up.

I know that because on Givr, one of the questions we ask before someone applies is whether they'll be available for the duration of the project.

Almost everyone says yes.

But between clicking "Apply" and the actual volunteering day, things can change.

That's completely understandable.

The difficult part isn't that someone can't make it anymore.

It's when no one knows.

Someone else is waiting for you before they can begin.

A task has been assigned to you.

Resources have been prepared.

The team is counting on the number of people who said they'd be there.

One simple message saying, "I won't be able to make it anymore." gives organisers a chance to plan differently.

You don't have to write a long explanation.

Just let them know.

Building Givr keeps teaching me different lessons.

One of them is that volunteering isn't only about caring for a cause—you also have to care for the people you're volunteering with.
