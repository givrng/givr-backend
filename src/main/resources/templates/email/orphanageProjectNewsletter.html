The Big 4 Are Not the Only Thing Orphanages Need.

Sometimes, the best way to help is simply to ask.

Hey there,

Not KPMG, Deloitte, PwC or EY. I mean the other Big 4: food, clothes, toys and books. (If I missed one, tell me.)

I know them well, because my first ever volunteering experience was an orphanage visit in 100 level, and kid you not, that's exactly what we carried. Big 4, no missing item. 😅

But I've never stopped thinking about it.

We took pictures, spent a few hours with the kids, and left. Nothing wrong with that. But people surely don't show up there every day.

So what if there were other things they needed and nobody asked?

What if one home needed a teacher?

What if another just needed someone who comes regularly, not once in December?

Birthdays and festive seasons don't come every week.

The idea behind this project came from a few of us, but that memory is a big part of why I can't let it go.

We won't know unless we ask.
So we're asking. 💙

Here's what we're doing 👇🏾

Givr is reaching out to orphanages to find out what they actually need, then putting them all in one network.

Anyone who wants to help can see which home needs what and where they'd be useful.

Because the usual outreach can have us feeling like we made a difference while missing the thing that would actually change something for those kids — and even the people taking care of them.

How we're doing it

1. We make first contact.
I reach out to the orphanage first and explain the idea.

2. They say yes.
Once they agree, we move to the next step.

3. A volunteer gets involved.
A volunteer close to the orphanage goes through a short questionnaire with their representative. Way lighter on you.

Why do I make first contact?

Because we tried sending volunteers with official items and it didn't go well.

Almost every orphanage wanted to talk to the founder directly. Lesson learned.

This new method has proven excellent.

The project is live on Givr. 💙

If you're in, go ahead and volunteer.
