Menstrual Health initiative Project 💙

“Some people burn their pads after use and some people wrap it in black leather does that mean they will use what they wrapped for ritual?”

“I heard that if you are on your period you don’t have to be playing rough with boys.”

Hello User,

These are things young girls in Nigeria believe about their own bodies.

Real notes from the girls myths, questions, and everything in between.

Two weeks ago, I attended the first session of the SHE Initiative’s menstrual health project not as a volunteer, but as the Co-founder of Givr. I wanted to see firsthand again what our platform is making happen on the ground.

The girls were so receptive, engaging and as a matter of fact they even picked their favorite volunteer. I can’t 😭. Anyway, their teacher was very warm and welcoming and the whole room had such good energy that I didn’t even want to leave.

There were so many activities introduced on day 1, but one stuck with me. It was so creative, hilarious and eye opening. The volunteers handed out sticky notes and asked each girl to write down anything anyone had ever told them about menstruation. Then they read each note out loud and went through which ones were myths and which were facts.

Some of what those girls had been told was hilarious and some pretty sad and the volunteers immediately debunked them. Watching the girls’ faces and reactions as they learned the truth was everything.

This is week three of a six-week program. The volunteers show up every week, one day at a time, and the girls are better for it. It’s an every Wednesday for 6 weeks program. Next week I’ll tell the story of this week.
